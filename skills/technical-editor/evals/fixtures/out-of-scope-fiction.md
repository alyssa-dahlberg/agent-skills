# The Lighthouse Keeper

The lamp turned. It had turned every night for forty years, and Aled had climbed to meet it every
one of them — up the one hundred and six steps, past the window where the gulls slept, into the
small hot room that smelled of oil and salt and the particular loneliness of a job done well.

He did not think of it as loneliness. He thought of it as the sea, and the light, and himself:
three things that had agreed, a long time ago, to keep each other company.

Tonight the wind came from the north, which meant the fishing boats would run late, which meant
he would stay awake until he counted all nine of them home. He always counted. Nine boats went
out. Nine boats came back. That was the arrangement, and Aled kept his half of it faithfully,
even on the nights when the sea did not.
